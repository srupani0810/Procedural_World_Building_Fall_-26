# Decay

A living conceptual position paper and design brief for this project. Read this before implementing any new feature, visual change, or system. Align the work to the stance and method below. Where a section is marked **open**, ask before inventing a settled claim.

This is a position on what decay *means* here — not a description of on-screen effects.

---

## Stance (settled so far)

**Decay, in this project, is abandonment under deindustrialization — imagined as a future condition, not a period recreation of the twentieth-century Rust Belt.**

The absence that drives decay is not catastrophe and not neutral aging. It is industry — and the maintenance labor that traveled with it — withdrawing. What remains is the **building as leftover industrial shell**: evidence of what was extracted and discarded. The structure does not vanish when the work leaves. It stays legible as infrastructure long after its use has gone. Time then works on that leftover form.

**Temporal frame (settled):** this is **future deindustrialization** — not a documentary set in a known past industrial region. Historical and technical research still informs *how* neglect cascades through buildings; it does not lock costume, region, or “nostalgic ruin” as the world. Ask before tying the project to a specific past place or decade as the setting.

**Driver of the absence (settled):** **rapid automation and AI**, not the classic offshoring story.

Historical deindustrialization often meant factories relocating to lower-wage countries — capital moved, jobs and the maintenance labor that followed those jobs left with them. The future shift this project imagines is **heavily accelerated by robotics, machine learning, and smart manufacturing that require fewer human workers per unit of output**. The withdrawal is therefore less “the plant moved overseas” and more **human labor (including the people who kept buildings standing) becoming surplus to production**. Shells accumulate where a human-scaled industrial footprint is no longer needed — even if output elsewhere continues under automation.

That tightens the leftover-shell claim: evidence of extraction and discard under a regime that can produce **without** the workforce that once also meant continuous upkeep. Concurrent growth elsewhere can read as attention and capital concentrating into leaner, automated capacity while older stock is left to time.

**Vacancy pattern (settled):** buildings are **gradually being vacated** — not a single shutdown day, and not “still fully running with nobody left to sweep.” Workforce and use thin out over time as automation raises output per remaining human; upkeep thins with them. Stages on a cell can rhyme with that taper: still almost held together while some care remains, then clearly left as presence and maintenance drop, then impacted by prolonged absence — always with the shell still there. Across the town, gradual vacating + concurrent growth = some lots emptying while others still receive attention.

So the claim is triple:

1. **Legibility after use.** Architecture persists as readable built form after productive use and the care that came with it have left. The shell is proof of discard, not a blank ruin-mood.
2. **Time as process on the leftover.** Neglect is staged. The building remains infrastructure while time alters it. Decay is what happens to what capital left behind when no upkeep replaces the labor that left with the job.
3. **Never completely discarded.** Real decayed infrastructure is the reference: architecture is *impacted* by abandonment and time — stained, weakened, partly taken by weather and ground — but it is not wiped from the world. Even late stages should read as **altered infrastructure still present**, not erasure of evidence, not a clean return to empty land. “Reclaimed” is pressure and absorption working on the shell, not deletion of the shell.

The design reference is **real-life decaying infrastructure**: how mass, aperture, structure, and ground actually change when upkeep stops. Use that impact logic — what persists, what fails first, what still declares “this was built” — rather than fantasy collapse or total dissolve.

**Reference class (soft):** a **mix** is welcome — mills/plants, yards and sheds, housing tied to industrial labor, civil pieces (bridges, canals, rails). No single type is locked as primary yet. Prefer shared laws of neglect across types (what fails first, what stays legible) over picking one picturesque typology. When a feature needs a concrete referent, ask which type fits that change.

**How impact shows (settled as mix):** decay reads through **several modes together**, not one “failure type” winning:
- **surface** — paint wear and tear, staining
- **material** — rust and corrosion
- **structure** — partial collapse at times (lean, missing span, broken bay) while the shell **still stands**

**Sequencing must follow research, not invent a pretty order.** When material wear vs geometric failure appears relative to stage (settled / abandoned / reclaimed), base it on how post-industrial vacancy and deferred maintenance actually cascade — deindustrialization as withdrawal of use and upkeep, then building physics under neglect. Use past cases as **process evidence** for a **future** setting: same neglect physics, not the same period look. Do not invent a hierarchy for drama. Do not flatten everything to simultaneous “ruin look” if sources describe a causal chain.

**Provisional research reading (candidate; lock sources with the author before treating as doctrine):**

Across industrial-heritage and vacant-building literature, a recurring chain after closure is:

1. **Use and maintenance labor leave** (the deindustrialization absence).
2. **Enclosure fails** — roofs, glazing, and openings stop being kept; water and weather enter. Case evaluations of vacated mills often name *negligence of a sound enclosure after vacancy* as the primary driver of later damage — not a single catastrophic event.
3. **Surface and material decay accelerate** — paint failure, staining, corrosion, rot, mold — while much of the mass still stands.
4. **Structural members weaken and partially fail** — floors, roofs, bays — often unevenly; masonry shells frequently outlast timber or steel interiors. Partial collapse appears as a *consequence of prolonged neglect*, years into vacancy, not as the opening image of abandonment.
5. **The shell often remains** — damaged, partly open, still readable as industrial infrastructure — matching this project’s “never completely discarded.”

So across a town at one moment, lots look like a **mix** (different times since care withdrew). On a *single* cell over time, research leans toward **enclosure/surface/material leading, partial structural failure later** — not rubble first. Strip-out (“urban mining”), vandalism, and contamination show up in real sites as accelerants after vacancy; treat those as open design questions, not automatic spectacle.

**Working implication until sources are locked:** prefer paint/rust and failed enclosure cues earlier in the stage sequence; introduce partial geometric collapse later and sparsely; never total wipe. Revisable when the author settles a research canon.

**Weathering v3 visual rule (settled):** never dissolve solid wall mass into a wireframe/lattice or wash the shell into terrain. Mass stays mass — upper stories stay square/plumb; wear is surface (paint loss, staining) and metal rust streaks, not warped masonry. Settled/abandoned lead with water-driven staining, paint chalking, and rust dripping from metal edges; reclaimed adds *localized* failure (sagging lintel / diagonal brace on one bay, cladding void, broken panes) plus sparse base scrap/debris — shell never erased.

**Concurrent growth** belongs in the same economy: capital and attention relocating. Some lots keep being claimed and stacked while others age into shells. That uneven field supports the leftover-shell claim — discard is selective; care moves elsewhere. In argument and in design emphasis, **lead with how decay unfolds on the abandoned cell**, then widen to growth relocating beside it. Do not open on bustling new construction and treat shells as backdrop.

## Against the clichés

This concept sits next to familiar traps. The position and the mechanics are meant to push against them — not decorate them.

| Cliché | Risk | Push (settled / partial) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ruins as spectacle | Photogenic empty factory; beauty of abandonment without claim | Shell as **evidence of extraction and discard**, not atmosphere. **Open:** how far visuals must refuse the picturesque. |
| Period cosplay | Future decay dressed as already-nostalgic Rust Belt / heritage ruin tourism | Frame is **future deindustrialization**. Past research teaches process; it does not set costume. |
| Offshoring rerun | Telling only the twentieth-century “factory left for cheaper labor abroad” story | Driver here is **automation/AI reducing workers per output**, not (primarily) geographic relocation of plants. |
| Decay as mood without consequence | Weathering as filter; nothing at stake | Decay is a **per-cell process with legible stages**, driven by absence of upkeep. Stages are consequences of neglect over time, not a look. Impact follows **real infrastructure under neglect**, not a style pass. |
| Abandonment as empty signifier for “loss” | Vague melancholy; no subject of what was lost | Loss is specific: **industrial use and its maintenance labor** withdrew; the shell is what that withdrawal left — and that shell **remains**. |
| Implied catastrophe | Storm, war, or sudden end that “explains” the ruin | No disaster event. Deindustrialization *is* the absence. The clock is upkeep not supplied — not a collapse. |
| Total wipe / romantic dissolve | Late decay as disappearing act; land “healed” clean | Architecture is **never completely discarded**. Late stage = impacted presence, not deletion. Partial collapse only — shell remains. |
| Single-channel “ruin look” | Only rust *or* only peeling paint *or* only rubble | Impact is a **mix**: paint wear, rust, and occasional partial collapse, uneven across lots. |

When a feature would make decay read as disaster theater, generic vibe, or pretty ruin, it conflicts with this brief.

## Method, tied to the position

The originality is not a feature list. It is how the system argues the stance.

### Decay as a systemic per-cell value

Each claimed cell carries age and derived stage (new → settled → abandoned → reclaimed). Wear is not a one-time art pass over a finished model. It is a **continuing value** that tracks absence of upkeep on that lot. That matches the claim that discard is process: the shell stays infrastructure while time and neglect rewrite it in place. Late stages must still argue **impacted presence** — ground and weather press on the form; the form does not finish disappearing.

### Absence of upkeep, not disaster

Nothing in the decay clock is a catastrophe trigger. Stages advance because care is not applied. That is the mechanical form of deindustrialization-as-absence: work and maintenance leave; the building remains and ages.

### Growth and decay running concurrently

The town can still claim new empty lots while other cells abandon and reclaim. That is capital/attention relocating — not a single end-state of ruin. Design should keep both readable, with **decay’s sequence primary** in how the concept is taught and how new systems are framed.

**Player agency (settled for Weathering v3):** the player may **only claim empty lots**. Existing shells cannot be cleared, demolished, restored, stacked further, or otherwise intervened on. A new claim starts held (stage new) and enters the same gradual, staggered vacating as everything else. Growth is adding automation-era structure into an already unevenly decaying field — not repairing or erasing leftovers.

### Uneven vacating, not a synchronized ruin clock

Vacating spreads at **per-cell offsets and rates from visible spatial position** (rim vs core, soft west→east bias) so a viewer can sense uneven automation adoption through the town — not one global countdown and not a hidden hash. A Phase 1 timeline intro may scrub history from an intact held city toward a **mixed** end state (held / thinning / vacated / impacted stages present together). That end is never “every cell impacted at once,” and no cell is wiped during the sequence; shells persist. Phase 2 continues aging from wherever each lot landed.

### Presence flicker (settled for Weathering v3)

Window-lamp flicker is a **legible presence signal**, not atmosphere noise. It appears **only on cells tagged `inhabited`**. Which lots are inhabited is a **central-share rule** (closest lots to origin fill up to the panel fraction) — not a per-cell hash. Timing of the on/off cycle is a **stable per-cell period/phase** (slow, duty from the panel) so the lamp reads as presence, not noise — evaluated from clock time, not re-randomized every frame. Vacated shells do not flutter. No barrel / candle / ground fires. State changes are logged as process events (`flicker on` / `flicker off`) so the behavior is demonstrable as designed.

### Architecture from neighbor rules, not hand-authored monuments

Houses emerge from grid, height, and adjacency (walls, arches, stairs, roofs). They are not authored ruin set-pieces. The leftover shell is therefore a **procedural industrial remainder** — form that came from settlement rules, then outlived the care that would have kept it “new.” That resists ruin-as-composition: the abandoned thing was produced by the same system that still grows elsewhere.

**Massing decisions are legible (settled for Weathering v3):** story count, swatch, roof kind, tarps, failure bay, inhabited tags, and idle-growth picks must come from **observable inputs** (neighbor configuration, height, distance/density rings, panel shares) — not per-cell hash rolls or `Math.random`. Every visual difference between two shells should be explainable in one sentence using something a viewer can see. Process-log lines state the rule used.

**Seed layout vacancies (settled for Weathering v3):** Phase 1 does **not** fill every disk cell. Corridors are **rule-based but uneven**: the same rim + west/north spatial signals as vacate clocks set per-line period and phase (tighter near the core, looser at the rim, shifted by direction) — not a fixed every-Nth graph-paper lattice. A **max-block floor** then forces a corridor at the midpoint of any axis-aligned claimed run longer than 3, so bias tightening cannot leave fused pockets larger than roughly 3×3. Empty seed lots stay claimable in Phase 2 (add-only). Tarps must not hang past one lot (`cellSize`) so they cannot visually bridge a street. Process log records each vacancy reason (bias vs max-block floor).

Background scenery and player lots both sit in this economy of attention; new work should not invent a separate “ruination aesthetic” that ignores the per-cell rule logic.

## Design brief rules

1. **Read this file first** before any new feature, visual change, or system.
2. **Align to stance and method.** If the work only adds “decay look,” it is incomplete.
3. **Lead with decay-as-process on the shell**, then with relocating growth — not the reverse.
4. **No catastrophe framing** unless the author explicitly revises this stance.
5. **Never fully discard architecture.** Prefer real decaying-infrastructure logic (what stays legible, what fails) over dissolve-to-nothing or empty-lot endpoints.
6. **Sequence impact from research** on deindustrialization / post-vacancy neglect (provisional chain above). Do not invent collapse timing for mood.
7. **Ask.** This paper is unfinished by design. Prefer a short question to the author over inventing settled theory, precedents, or visual doctrine for open sections.
8. **Update this file** when a design conversation settles a new claim, so the brief stays the source of truth.

## Working summary

Future deindustrialization — driven by robotics, ML, and smart manufacturing that need fewer workers per output — withdraws human use and upkeep **gradually**. Buildings empty over time as labor thins; they are not deleted in a single event. The leftover shell is evidence of extraction and discard under that taper. Architecture is never completely discarded: real decaying infrastructure (studied as process, not period costume) shows impact (paint wear, rust, occasional partial collapse — a mix), not deletion. Growth elsewhere is attention and automated capacity relocating. The system encodes this as per-cell absence of care, not disaster; as concurrent growth and decay, not a final ruin tableau; as rule-built architecture that outlasts the labor that would have maintained it.
